# Caught in the Net

Every now and then I'll spend five minutes looking for a single frame.

Usually it's a frame that goes by in a fraction of a second.

I'll scrub backwards and forwards through a scene by milliseconds until I find it.

Sometimes I can't explain why I stopped there either. The smallest change in someone's expression, or the point at which a movement begins or ends, can completely change the feeling of the image.

Move forward a frame and it's gone.

Move back one and it hasn't shown itself yet.

I started seeking them out more when I started working on Quiet Ruin.

I'd be watching a film or a tv show and suddenly see something I could imagine sitting alongside the music.

Since then, I've found myself watching certain things differently. Sometimes I can tell almost immediately that there's something in there worth looking for.

A photo already knows what it is.

Someone has already decided that this was the image worth keeping.

Every moving image contains thousands of images nobody chooses to see.

They just pass too quickly to ever be seen on their own.

The difference between one frame and the next is almost nothing. Sometimes I can't explain why one feels right and another doesn't.

They're separated by milliseconds, but they can feel like completely different images.

I remember stopping on a scene where a woman throws a bar stool at a mirror. I went back and forth until I found the moment the glass was breaking apart, her face caught mid-scream behind it.

The combination of everything happening at once created a mood of its own.

When I stop on one of those frames, it becomes something in its own right, something that was never meant to be seen that way.
