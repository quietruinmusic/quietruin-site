# Afterimage

Eventually, I've got to let the record go.

I've spent all that time working on it, deep in the details, trying to get every part of it where I need it to be.

I know what the record means to me.

But I can't decide what somebody else hears in it.

Their own experiences will shape that.

They might love it.

They might hate it.

They might think it's alright and never listen to it again.

They might hear something in it that I never intended or expected.

After that, I've done what I can.

I like that, even if it can be strange.

Then there comes a point where there is nothing left to do.

I've listened to the songs so many times that I can barely hear them properly anymore.

I'm too close to everything.

I know where every guitar part came from. I remember every version of the mix. I know the tiny details that nobody else is going to notice.

For a long time, that's all I can hear.

Then I finish it.

And I can step back.

The magnifying glass comes away.

I can listen to it more like a music fan again.

Not completely, obviously.

But I'm not as lost in the weeds.

I can hear it for what it is.

And then there is the physical record.

Seeing something on a screen is one thing. Holding it in your hands is another.

The songs are there.

The artwork is there.

All the separate parts have become one thing.

The whole thing you had in your head has become an actual object.

There is a bit of pride in that.

A bit of joy.

And a sense of peace.
