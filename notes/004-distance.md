# Distance

Every so often I'll go back to a record I haven't listened to in years.

The music hasn't changed, but I have.

There are albums I thought were masterpieces when I first heard them. I go back now and realise they were inseparable from the time I experienced them.

The excitement, the people I was around, what else I was listening to, who I wanted to be. The cloud of the time it was released in lifts and you're just left with the actual substance.

I've gone back to films years later and found things I'd overlooked the first time. They hadn't resonated because I hadn't lived enough life yet. Other films that felt profound don't carry the same weight anymore.

A few years ago, I went back to York on my own. I was there to scatter my dad's ashes with my sister. I hadn't been able to make it back for his funeral during Covid.

I was staying with a mate who loves a lie in, so one morning I got up early and went for a walk. I spent hours going around the city, visiting as many places as I could remember.

At one point I found myself back outside the house my mum rented for us on Kyme Street in Bishophill.

The houses are small, two-bedroom terraces joined tightly together. When I lived there, my bedroom was in the conservatory at the back, turned into a third bedroom.

From the front, almost nothing had changed.

I was looking at the same front door. The same houses.

But they didn't belong to me anymore.

It wasn't the sort of nostalgia that makes you wish you could go back.

I was glad I'd lived there, but I understood that it had become part of a life I couldn't come back to.

I went back to other places from my life as well. The village I grew up in. A few of the pubs I used to drink in. So much of it was still there.

I just wasn't the same person who'd left.

When I finish a record and put it out, I leave it as it is. I may make different decisions now, I may not, but the record belongs to the person who made it then.

Going back and changing it would somehow make it less honest. It would erase the evidence of who I was when I made it.

We're so willing to let old records, films and photos belong to another time, but not always the people we were.
