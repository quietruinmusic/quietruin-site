# The Bug

There are periods where I don't feel particularly creative.

I'll still think about music all the time, but that doesn't mean I can sit down and write something.

I can pick up the guitar and play for five minutes, then get fed up and put it back down. I can open Ableton, work on something for twenty minutes, realise it isn't working and have to leave it there.

That happened recently with a song I've been working on. I'd been playing a guitar part on the acoustic and had an idea of how it would work against a synth line I'd already recorded. When I finally got it into Ableton, it sounded nothing like I thought it would. I'd imagined the two parts balancing each other out. Instead, it sounded off-key and strange.

I didn't have time to sit there and fix it. I had to go to work, so I left it.

Even when I don't feel like working on music, I still seem to find ways of keeping the project moving.

I'll listen to demos in the car and try lyric ideas over the instrumental. Sometimes I'll tell myself I'm just going to watch TV, then end up half-listening while playing the guitar.

I think about Quiet Ruin from when I wake up until I go to sleep, even if it's somewhere in the background.

That's the problem with the bug.

It's a good bug.

Sometimes you're on a roll and ideas are coming quickly. Other times there's nothing there. You can sit down and try, but the juices just aren't flowing.

If I go too long without putting something into the project, I start to feel it.

Boredom. Frustration. Anxiety.

There are plenty of things I enjoy doing, but this feels like something I need to do.

When it's working, it hits all the pleasure centres in my brain.

I love figuring things out and making something out of nothing. It's a beautiful thing.

I didn't start out expecting to be able to do any of this.

I never thought I'd learn Ableton and produce my own records. I never thought I'd make an EP. I definitely never thought I'd make an album I was genuinely proud of.

For a long time, part of the drive was proving to myself that I could do it.

If I'm being honest, probably proving it to other people as well.

That I didn't need someone else to make it happen.

I could learn what I needed to learn and make the thing myself.

When I signed off the final mixes for in the space between, I knew I'd made the record I wanted to make.

Then I got the artwork back and saw that what I was trying to express in the music had translated into something visual.

I'd done the thing I'd spent years wondering if I was capable of doing.

I could probably be happy with that record for a long time.

Maybe forever. I don't know.

But I also have a feeling that eventually I'll want to do another one.

Maybe even sooner than I think.

The bug doesn't really get better.

It just changes.

I went hard making the EP and then in the space between. Afterwards, I was knackered.

So I'm trying to do things differently now.

There's always that voice, though.

Get it done.

Produce it.

Mix it.

Master it.

Put it out.

Make the video.

Get the artwork sorted.

I'm trying to quieten that down.

For now, I'm happy to let things take their time.

But for how long?
