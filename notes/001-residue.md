# Residue

I used to listen to techno in bed in the dark before going to sleep.

It became a habit in different places. Leeds when I was at university. York afterwards. Then London.

Most people associate that kind of music with clubs and being surrounded by other people, but for me it was almost the opposite.

I'd put my headphones on, switch the lights off and lie on my back with my eyes closed in the dark. Sometimes I'd be half in and half out of sleep. Sometimes I'd fall asleep to it. Other times I'd lie there for hours, listening to particular mixes until I was completely immersed in them.

It wasn't really about dancing.

That could happen in a room full of people too. I'd felt it in clubs, surrounded by people, with the music being the only thing I could focus on.

A lot of the music I tend to get lost in has something hypnotic about it.

Small changes start to matter. A note might shift slightly, or a sound might appear that wasn't there before and you really feel the impact.

It's the same reason I like films that take their time. When nothing is rushing to the next thing, you have more time to notice the finer details.

I've found that feeling in all sorts of different places over the years.

Looking back, I've always been searching for the same thing.

Somewhere you know that you're happy to stay for a while.

When something really grabs me, I'll listen to it again and again. Sometimes forty or fifty times. It's easy to lose count.

I don't consciously think about any of this when I'm writing music.

But that's really what I'm trying to make.

Something people might want to spend a little more time with.
